#### **2. French Real Estate Market & Competitive Landscape**
- **Market Structure:** The French real estate market is divided into private (offices, housing, retail) and public/social housing. The company is primarily focused on social housing.
- **Private Real Estate:** This segment is dominated by Sopra Steria's Alta X solution (~80% market share), making it difficult for competitors to enter. Other players include listed real estate companies (SFEI), large developers, and institutional users.
- **Social Housing:** This market is a duopoly between Sopra Steria and Arian. It is characterized by high dissatisfaction with current providers due to high costs and low innovation, creating demand for alternatives. Approximately 95-98% of social housing companies are equipped with an ERP, but major vendors are moving legacy on-premise systems to new SaaS platforms, causing customer frustration.
- **Market Trends:** There is significant customer interest in CRM and AI solutions. Companies are adopting a "best of breed" strategy, adding specialized solutions to their existing IT infrastructure. The French market is also experiencing a shortage of property managers and high employee rotation, increasing the demand for guided, automated IT systems.
#### **3. Company Product Suite and Strategy**
- **Core Offerings:** The company's product line covers the tenant lifecycle, including matching (MO), voting (Dematimo), rent contracts (Enelock), CRM (Soli), and debt collection (Arilog). Arilog is the current bestseller. The company is strong in matching and debt collection but weaker in CRM and rent contracts.
- **Product Demonstration (Emold, Dimatimo, Entrelac):**
  - **Emold:** Manages available flats, creates and publishes ads to listing sites (Leboncoin, Se loger), manages prospects, and uses a rule-based system to match candidates from the SNE tenant applicant list.
  - **Dimatimo:** A platform to facilitate the "very political" committee review process for tenant selection, supporting both live screen-sharing and remote voting.
  - **Entrelac:** A portal for both employees and tenants, guiding the tenant journey from selection to lease signing and supporting both online and offline processes.
- **Product Scope & UI:** The team plans a UI overhaul and needs to decide between using a high-resolution UI or the existing Habison design system. The platform manages tenants with extensive filters, proactive BPM-triggered actions (emails/SMS), and financial tracking.
- **AI Adoption:** The supervisory board is pushing for AI investment for productivity gains, but this arose after budgets were set. There is concern about the rising costs of usage-based pricing models (e.g., tokens for GitHub Copilot), which could run into tens of thousands of euros monthly.
#### **4. Technical Architecture and ERP Integration Challenges**
- **Core Challenge:** A major blocker is the inability to easily write data back into clients' closed ERP systems. Current workarounds like RPA (UiPath) are too costly for many organizations. This dependency limits market access and poses a risk to the "best of breed" strategy.
- **Data Integration:** Reading data from client databases is feasible, but ERPs are not live systems (payments sync 1-2 times daily). Data models require per-customer mapping, as definitions (e.g., "rent") vary even within the same ERP.
- **International Architecture:** The product for the French market will be a layer on top of a core international version being built for Denmark. Country-specific functionality is handled either in the core code or via country-specific apps that inject UI into the main platform, using "master instances" for legislative settings.
- **MVP Roadmap:** An MVP is planned for 2027, including demand management, billing, debt recovery, and work requests. Technical components like AI and data access are planned for the end of 2026.
#### **5. France-Specific Housing Processes and Requirements**
- **Tenant Allocation:** In French social housing, a committee allocates apartments to one of three selected candidates. This contrasts with the waiting-list-based systems in Denmark/Netherlands. The system must also manage "reservors" (e.g., the state, Action Logement) who have rights to a percentage of units.
- **Rent Collection & Eviction:** The French process is slow and highly protective of tenants, with evictions prohibited in winter and taking 1-3 years. This contrasts sharply with Denmark, where rent payment is highly automated and subsidized by the government for the unemployed.
- **Mandatory Government Reporting & Integrations:** The system must support numerous French-specific requirements:
  - **Surveys:** Annual SLS survey (income check) and OPS survey (housing stock occupation), including billing penalties for non-compliance.
  - **Connections:** Integration with government systems like CAF/APL (rent support), RPLS (national property registry), SNE (tenant directory), and FranceConnect.
  - **Reporting:** "Convention d’utilité Sociale" (CUS) reporting on commitments to local authorities.
- **Asset Hierarchy:** The system must model a complex asset hierarchy (property/group, building, entrance, unit) with inconsistent terminology used by clients ("réseau," "groupe," "ensemble"). It also needs to handle mixed ownership and owners' associations for managing shared costs.
- **Language:** All software and client engagement must be in French. The current system is only in French, but internationalization is being considered for broader European expansion.
